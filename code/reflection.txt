In history.py, I learned to clean whole columns with vectorized .str methods
instead of apply. In clean_employee_id, "E4" needed .str[1:].str.zfill(3), not
just .str.upper(), to match the roster. clean_number used
pd.to_numeric(errors="coerce") with .fillna(0.0), and parse_pay_date used two
pd.to_datetime passes with a format, joined by fillna, so unreadable dates
became NaT. combine_exports showed why pd.concat needs ignore_index=True.

The key idea was grain. add_clean_columns and add_roster follow lineage (copy,
add, return), but summarize_employees in summarize.py uses a group by with named
aggregation to build a new table. In reshape.py, missed_weeks used hours_grid's
pivot_table with fill_value=0, then melt, to turn absences into real rows.

I struggled with reset_index: in summarize_employees I needed drop=True after
sort_values to avoid an extra column. pivot_table arguments in pay_by_month also
took help. Next, I will rebuild the filters in payroll_report_app.py from
scratch and practice pivot_table and melt on a new dataset, using explore.py to
check each step.
